**Beginners in the Herd: Did ChatGPT Increase Stock-Market Herding When New Investors Poured In?**

<!-- abstract start -->
Herding means investors copy each other instead of using their own information. A recent study found that ChatGPT made the average retail investor less herd-prone, but beginners might treat a chatbot's near-identical answers as advice and herd more. We tested whether herding rose after ChatGPT's release (November 30, 2022) during periods of heavy new-investor entry. We used daily returns of Russell 1000 stocks from 2016 to 2026 (2.26 million stock-days) and an entry index from Google Trends and brokerage account data. We measured herding with two published tests, the standard dispersion (CSAD) test and Wang and Hudson's signed version, pre-registering each later check before running it.

The hypothesis was not supported. The key coefficient was negative but not significant (one-sided p = 0.053; 0.20 with corrected standard errors). Herding also rose in low-entry periods, and the effect appeared equally at 84 fake break dates (p = 0.43). Because the two tests disagreed on whether markets herd, we simulated 80,000 markets with known herding. In realistic markets with no herding, the Wang-Hudson test reported herding 100% of the time, against 0.1% for the standard test. We traced the failure to a jump in the signed measure where the market return crosses zero, confirmed it in the paper's exact method, and showed the paper's own simulation hid it with unusually large market moves.

We found no evidence that ChatGPT increased herding among new investors, and the Wang-Hudson test should not be relied on for daily stock data in its published form.
