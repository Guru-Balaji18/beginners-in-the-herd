## 1. Hypotheses, exactly as written in the original research plan

**H1.** The nonlinear CSAD coefficient becomes significantly more negative after November 30, 2022, but only in high entry periods. In the model below, the coefficient on the three way interaction is negative and statistically significant.

**H2.** In the post-ChatGPT period during low entry, herding is unchanged or weaker than in the matched pre-period. This is the condition under which our result is compatible with Even-Tov et al. rather than contradicting them.

**H3.** The effect is concentrated in stocks with high retail attention and is much smaller or absent in stocks held mostly by institutions.

**Null hypothesis.** The three way interaction coefficient is zero. ChatGPT’s release did not change the relationship between new investor entry and herding.

Also from the plan, unchanged:

- *Primary test:* "A one sided t test on g5 against the null that it is zero, at alpha equal to 0.05."
- *Falsification:* "a three way interaction coefficient that is zero, positive, or negative but not statistically distinguishable from zero once we control for volatility and market stress. A negative coefficient that also appears at randomly chosen placebo break dates would mean we are picking up a trend rather than an event, and we would treat that as a failed result too. We plan to report the result either way."

## 2. Results so far (honest summary)

All numbers are from `data/step5_main_results.csv` and
`data/step4_h3_results.csv`, run 2026-09-23 on the corrected panel
(`return_panel_final.csv`, after fixing 36 ticker renames). Newey-West
HAC standard errors, 5 lags.

**Primary specification** (fixed before any regression output was seen,
but after the entry-index diagnostics; it deviates from the Aug-31 plan
as listed in section 5): no Robinhood component, growth-rate entry
components, High/Low tercile dummies with the middle third as baseline,
expanding tercile cutoffs.

| Test | g5 (Post x High) | SE | two-sided p | one-sided p (plan's test) | Post x Low | two-sided p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard CCK | -3.99 | 2.47 | 0.106 | 0.053 | -6.15 | 0.031 |
| Wang-Hudson SCSAD | -430.5 | 153.6 | 0.005 | 0.003 | -767.7 | <0.001 |

- **H1: weak and fragile.** The plan's one-sided test on the standard
  model gives p = 0.053, not significant at 0.05. Wang-Hudson gives
  p = 0.003. Across all 25 specification variants, 10 have g5 with
  two-sided p < .05, **6 negative and 4 positive**. The four
  continuous-index Wang-Hudson variants are positive and significant,
  the opposite of H1.
- **H2: contradicted.** The Post x Low term is negative and significant
  in both tests, as large as or larger than Post x High: herding-like
  curvature rose in low-entry periods too.
- **H3: significant in the opposite direction.** Standard test,
  difference in g5 (high-attention stocks minus low-attention stocks) =
  +2.92, SE 0.87, two-sided p = 0.0008. The effect is stronger in
  low-attention stocks. Wang-Hudson difference = +50.4, p = 0.14, same
  direction.
- **Calendar confound.** After ChatGPT the entry terciles are almost
  pure year blocks: Low = all of 2023 (250/250 days) plus Dec 2022,
  High = only 2025-26, Middle = mostly 2024-25. So g5 and the Post x Low
  term largely compare years with each other.
- **Base herding disagreement** (no High/Low terms): the standard test
  finds none (quadratic term p = 0.95), while Wang-Hudson finds strong
  herding (cubic term p < 0.0001).

## 3. Part B — Monte Carlo simulation of the two tests (pre-specified)

**Question.** On data where we *know* whether herding exists, how often
does each test say "herding"? This tells us which real-data result to
trust.

**B1. Tests (unchanged code).**
- `standard_cck_regression()` and `wang_hudson_scsad_regression()` from
  `robustness_wang_hudson.py` are called as-is.
- CSAD and SCSAD come from `compute_csad()` in `build_csad.py`, as-is.
- Same Newey-West HAC standard errors, 5 lags.
- Both functions include a Post interaction term. So the regression call
  is identical to the real one, each simulated sample gets a Post dummy
  on its last share of days equal to the real share on/after 2022-11-30.
- If `compute_csad()` is too slow at this scale, the loop may use a
  numpy version of the same formula, but only if
  `test_simulate_herding_tests.py` shows it matches `compute_csad()` to
  within 1e-12 on simulated panels. The regression functions are never
  rewritten.

**B2. Calibration** (`data/sim_calibration.json`), from
`return_panel_final.csv` / `market_returns_final.csv`:
- median stocks per day (N) and number of trading days (T);
- each stock's beta on the equal-weighted market return (stocks with at
  least 250 days), and its residual volatility sigma_i, saved as
  (beta, sigma) pairs;
- volatility co-movement: the daily cross-sectional standard deviation
  of residuals, s_t, regressed on |R_m,t|: s_t = a + b|R_m,t|;
- residual excess kurtosis (for reference);
- the real daily equal-weighted market return series;
- the real base-herding statistics recomputed with the same two
  functions on the corrected panel (coefficient, t, p for the CCK
  quadratic term and the Wang-Hudson cubic term).

**B3. Data-generating process**, per simulated market:
1. Draw the input market series R_m (length T) by moving-block bootstrap
   of the real series, 21-day blocks.
2. Draw N stocks with (beta_i, sigma_i) for the scenario.
3. Draw idiosyncratic shocks: eps_i,t = sigma_i * m_t * z_i,t.
   - z is standard normal, or Student-t with df = 4 rescaled to unit
     variance.
   - m_t = 1, or m_t = (a + b|R_m,t|) / (a + b * mean|R_m|).
4. Returns: R_i,t = R_m,t + (beta_i R_m,t + eps_i,t - R_m,t) * h_t,
   with h_t = 1 / (1 + kappa R_m,t^2). kappa = 0 reproduces the
   no-herding model R_i,t = beta_i R_m,t + eps_i,t exactly.
5. Mimic the real pipeline:
   - winsorize at the pooled 0.1 and 99.9 percentiles;
   - the market return the tests see is the equal-weighted mean of the
     simulated (winsorized) returns, as in `build_panel.py`.

**B4. Scenarios.**

| Scenario | betas | sigma_i | shocks z | vol multiplier m_t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 "textbook" | all 1 | median sigma for every stock | normal | 1 |
| S2 heterogeneous betas | real distribution | median sigma | normal | 1 |
| S3 fat tails | all 1 | median sigma | Student-t, df = 4 | 1 |
| S4 volatility clustering | all 1 | median sigma | normal | calibrated a, b |
| S5 "realistic" | real (beta, sigma) pairs | real | Student-t, df = 4 | calibrated a, b |

**B5. kappa grid and repetitions.**
- kappa in {0, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000, 3000}, chosen to span
  barely-detectable to strong. For scale: on a 3% market day,
  h = 0.97 at kappa = 30, 0.53 at kappa = 1000, and 0.27 at
  kappa = 3000.
- 1,000 simulated markets per scenario per kappa (40,000 total).
- Fixed seeds: `SeedSequence([20260928, scenario, rep])`, the same for
  every kappa within a scenario. These common random numbers make the
  power curves comparable across kappa.
- Results are appended per repetition to a CSV, so a crash resumes
  without redoing work.

**B6. Decision rules.**
- *"Herding detected"* = one-sided test at 5%: t < -1.645 on the CCK
  quadratic coefficient, or on the Wang-Hudson cubic coefficient.
  (A sign check plus two-sided p < .05 would have a 2.5% null rate, not
  5%. The plan's primary test is one-sided.)
- *Size* = detection rate at kappa = 0. With 1,000 repetitions the
  Monte Carlo standard error is about 0.7 points, so:
  - well-sized: 3.6% to 6.4%;
  - over-rejects: above 6.4%;
  - conservative: below 3.6%.
- *Power* = detection rate at each kappa > 0.
- *Sign check:* at kappa = 3000 in S1, both mean coefficients must be
  negative. Otherwise our sign convention is wrong, and we say so.
- *Matching reality:* compute the median simulated
  (t_CCK, t_WH) for each scenario and kappa. The "best match" is the
  cell nearest the real pair, by Euclidean distance. We also report the
  percentile of each real t-statistic in each scenario's kappa = 0
  distribution.
- *Which real result to trust:*
  1. If Wang-Hudson over-rejects in S5, its real p < 0.0001 is **not**
     evidence of herding by itself. It counts as evidence only if the
     real Wang-Hudson t-statistic is below the 5th percentile of S5's
     kappa = 0 Wang-Hudson t-statistics.
  2. If CCK is conservative or low-powered in S5 at the kappa that best
     matches reality, the real CCK p = 0.95 is **not** evidence against
     herding.
  3. If both tests are well-sized in S5, trust the more powerful one. If
     CCK is well-sized and Wang-Hudson over-rejects, trust CCK.
  4. These conclusions are reported whichever way they go.

**B7. Stated before running.**
- The SCSAD sign flip creates a jump of about 2 x CSAD at R_m = 0. The
  least-squares fit of an odd step function on R_m and R_m^3 has a
  **negative** cubic coefficient. For R_m ~ N(0,1) and target sign(x),
  the cubic coefficient is about -0.13.
- So our Wang-Hudson implementation may say "herding" at kappa = 0 even
  in S1. If the validation test fails for this reason, that is recorded
  as a result. We will not change the Wang-Hudson specification to make
  it pass.
- We implemented Wang-Hudson from the description in this project's
  notes and could not check it against the paywalled paper, for example
  whether it suppresses the intercept or adds a sign dummy. Conclusions
  apply to **our implementation**, which is what produced the real-data
  result.

**B8. Validation first** (`test_simulate_herding_tests.py`, before the full run):
- S1 at kappa = 0: size near 5% for both tests (1% to 10% with 200
  repetitions);
- at kappa = 3000: both tests detect herding in at least 95% of runs;
- the same seed reproduces results exactly.

**B9. Outputs:**
- `results/sim_size_power.csv`;
- a size-table figure;
- power curves, one panel per scenario.

## 4. Part C — placebo break dates (pre-specified)

**C1.** Re-run the primary specification exactly as in
`main_regression.py`, for both CCK and Wang-Hudson. Same entry index,
controls and HAC(5). Only Post_t changes: Post_t = 1 if date >= fake
date, with every interaction recomputed.

**C2. Fake dates.**
- Every calendar month-end from 2017-06-30 to 2025-06-30.
- Excluded: any date within 182 days of 2022-11-30.
- *Deviation from the original plan:* the plan said "200 randomly
  chosen dates". The month-end grid covers the same span, but it's
  deterministic and reproducible, and avoids near-duplicate dates. That
  said, adjacent placebo dates still share most of their data, so the
  placebo estimates are not independent draws.

**C3.** Record g5 and the Post x Low coefficient (estimate, SE, p) for
every fake date, for both tests.

**C4. Empirical p-value** = share of placebo g5 values <= the real-date
g5 (one-sided, in H1's direction). Also reported: the share of placebo
dates with a negative g5 that is one-sided significant at 5%, which is
the plan's own wording.

**C5. Decision rule.** If the real-date g5 is not more negative than at
least 95% of placebo-date g5 values (empirical p > 0.05), we conclude the
"effect" is **not specific to ChatGPT's release**. Applied separately to
CCK and Wang-Hudson.

**C6. Year fixed effects.**
- (a) Add calendar-year dummies to the controls.
- (b) Also add R_m^2 x year interactions (R_m^3 x year for Wang-Hudson),
  so g5 is identified only from **within-year** differences between
  High and Low days.
- Report how much High/Low variation remains within years: 1 - R^2 from
  regressing each dummy on year dummies, for all days and for
  post-ChatGPT days.
- *Decision:* if g5 under (b) is not negative and one-sided significant
  at 5%, conclude the primary g5 was driven by between-year differences
  (the calendar confound).

**C7. Multiple comparisons.** Holm-Bonferroni across the Part C and D
confirmatory-style tests (CCK and Wang-Hudson x {placebo p, year-FE (a),
year-FE (b), plan-literal g5} = 8 tests), as the plan specifies.
Reported raw and adjusted.

**C8. Outputs:** histogram of placebo g5 with the real date marked, and
a line plot of g5 across fake dates, for both tests.

## 5. Part D — plan-literal specification check (pre-specified)

The Aug-31 plan defines "High_t as one on high entry days and zero on
low entry days", a High-versus-Low comparison. Our primary spec instead
compares High with the middle third. We will run the plan-literal
version:
- only High and Low days kept;
- a single High dummy;
- primary index settings;
- both tests;
- g5 reported with the plan's one-sided test.

**Stated before running:** from the existing primary estimates, this
should come out near g5 - (Post x Low) = -3.99 - (-6.15) = **+2.16**
(CCK) and -430.5 - (-767.7) = **+337** (Wang-Hudson). That's the
opposite sign from H1. If confirmed, the plan-literal primary test
fails H1.

## 6. Other deviations from the Aug-31 plan, already made and documented in `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md`

- Robinhood excluded from the primary entry index. Its history starts
  mid-surge, which distorts past-only z-scores.
- Expanding tercile cutoffs instead of full-sample ones, to avoid
  lookahead.
- Entry components use growth rates, to remove firm-size trends.
- A Low_t term was added so H2 could be tested directly.
- The Wang-Hudson test was added as a robustness check.
- Retail attention is classified once, over the 52 weeks before
  ChatGPT, instead of monthly. FINRA's free data starts 2021-12. The
  Google Trends component could not be completed (throttling), so the
  index uses 2 of 3 components.
- The "top decile of realized volatility days" control is implemented
  as the top decile of |R_m,t|.
- FINRA's weekly non-ATS off-exchange data is used instead of the daily
  short-sale files, because non-ATS volume separates retail
  internalization from dark pools.
