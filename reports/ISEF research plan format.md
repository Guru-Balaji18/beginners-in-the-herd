# Freeze the August plan, then amend it openly

The research plan for all three fairs uses one skeleton: the 2026-2027 ISEF Form 1A "Research Plan/Project Addendum". It has five lettered parts: **a. Rationale; b. Research Question(s)/Hypothesis(es)/Engineering Goal(s)/Expected Outcomes; c. List of Materials, Procedures/Methodology, Risk and Safety, Data Analysis; d. Acknowledgements (including AI documentation); e. Bibliography** ([ISEF Form 1A](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)). Florida's SSEF adds stricter rules on top. The plan must be replicable, must name who does each step and where, needs **at least five non-rulebook sources** with full citations for any data repository, must record AI use in the bibliography, and **"must not include data, conclusions, or unapproved procedures"** ([2026-27 SSEF Rules Supplement](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ImzJHKi8CdMCD2b_YQWpJaAhyEbQcH74/view)). For your team, this settles the main question. Leave the August 2026 plan as written, in future tense and with no results. Append a dated addendum that records every change of method and the methodological reason for it, and keep findings out of it. The rule that applies to you is that changes made "before competing at an affiliated fair... must be added as an addendum to the original plan" ([Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)). After the Hillsborough fair on **February 17, 2027**, the project "may not be changed or amended." Hillsborough also wants the typed plan, the addendum and the abstract uploaded by **January 11, 2027** ([HCPS 6-12 Timeline](https://www.hillsboroughschools.org/page/stem-fair-grades-6-12-timeline/)), so the methods are effectively locked in eight weeks from now. Judges score the science through a 100-point rubric that has no mathematics-specific version. Public examples of winning work are abstracts only. I found no winner's actual research plan published anywhere.

## One ISEF skeleton governs all three fairs, with Florida's rules layered on top

**ISEF sets the base structure.** The 2026-2027 instructions say a plan "is required for ALL projects" and "must be written BEFORE experimentation." They list the five lettered parts above, and part c has four sub-bullets ([ISEF Form 1A](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)). The official documents contain two quirks worth knowing. First, the description under item b is a word-for-word copy of item a's. This looks like an editing error. The Companion Guide gives the useful prompt for item b: "How is this based on the rationale described above?" ([Companion Guide pp.10-11](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)). Second, the rule text in the body of the Rules Book lists only seven elements (a-g) and leaves out Acknowledgements ([Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)), while Form 1A includes it. A plan with an Acknowledgements section satisfies both versions. The document was renamed this year from "Research Plan/Project Summary" to "Research Plan/Project Addendum", and a few passages still use the old name.

**ISEF sets no length limit and no header block.** Identifying details such as names, title, school, adult sponsor, dates and **item 10 "Source of Data"** (where you can check "publicly available") go on the fixed Form 1A. The plan itself is a free-format attachment ([ISEF Form 1A](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)). Two passages in the Companion Guide matter for your team. Mathematics projects "will be less detailed in the initial project plan and will change through the course of research." The Guide also rejects the stock math risk answer ("papercuts or eye strain") and asks students to consider "the potential risks in their data management and to any psychological or privacy concerns" ([Companion Guide pp.10, 12](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)). If the project analyzes preexisting public datasets, it falls under the exempt "data/record review" category (the rules' examples are "baseball statistics, crime statistics"). It then needs no IRB pre-approval, but like every project it must still pass SRC review after experimentation and before competition ([Rules Book pp.8, 22](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)).

**SSEF Florida adopts the ISEF rules in full and adds stricter ones that "take precedence"** ([SSEF Rules & FAQs](https://ssefflorida.com/rules/)). The 2026-27 supplement goes beyond ISEF page 32 in several ways. It requires a "detailed step-by-step description" that is "fully and clearly replicable by a reviewer." It requires a clear split between student and adult steps (it suggests "Role of Student" and "Role of Mentor" headings) and a statement of where each phase happens and who supervises it. It requires an addendum for any "substantial addition, deletion, or clarification." The bibliography needs at least five sources, and data from open-access repositories needs a **complete citation, "not simply a URL"**. AI use must be recorded in the bibliography. A project submitted without a complete plan "will not be approved for competition... resubmissions will not be permitted" ([2026-27 SSEF Rules Supplement](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ImzJHKi8CdMCD2b_YQWpJaAhyEbQcH74/view)). The five-source minimum is new this year; the 2025-26 supplement did not have it ([2025-26 SSEF Supplement](https://www.stjohns.k12.fl.us/stem-fair/wp-content/uploads/sites/182/2025/07/2025-26-SSEF-Rules-Supplement.pdf)). Every SSEF abstract must be on the **official 72nd SSEF Abstract Form**.

**Hillsborough publishes no plan template of its own.** It requires **Forms 1, 1A (with the plan), 1B, 2A and 3 for every project** ([HR Paperwork Requirements](https://sites.google.com/view/hillsborough-stem-fair/hrsef-paperwork-requirements)). The Form 3 risk assessment, completed with the Direct Supervisor before experimentation, is stricter than ISEF practice for a pure data project. The only formatting instruction is that the uploaded plan be typed with "all template formatting and notes... removed" ([HCPS 6-12 Timeline](https://www.hillsboroughschools.org/page/stem-fair-grades-6-12-timeline/)). That wording suggests a template reaches students through teachers or the adult-sponsor training, but I could not find it online. On category, SSEF's **Mathematics and Computational Sciences (MACO)** has a "Probability and Statistics" subcategory covering "statistical tools used to analyze and interpret data." Hillsborough points students to SSEF's category definitions ([SSEF Categories](https://ssefflorida.com/categories/)). At ISEF the matching category is Mathematics (MATH), and judges are "assigned based on their expertise in your chosen subcategory" ([ISEF Categories](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/categories-and-subcategories/)).

**Formatting conventions.** No ISEF or SSEF document sets a font, a citation style or a page count. The conventions that hold up come from the rules' own wording and from fair-level guidance. Use the official lettered headings in the official order: the rules require digitally generated forms to have "the same content and order as ISEF forms" ([Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)). Write the plan in **future tense**; the 2026-27 SSEF supplement switched its own sample wording to "will be performed." Use numbered procedure steps so a reviewer can replicate them. Type everything and strip all template prompts before uploading. The University of Tennessee's Southern Appalachian fair is one published example of fair-level guidance. It asks for an outline format with lettered headings, complete sentences, "at least 5 reputable source[s]" in APA, and a header with Name(s), Project Title, School and Date, and it reuses the same form as the Addendum ([SASEF Research Plan](https://sasef.utk.edu/research-plan/)). APA is a reasonable default, but it is a convention, not an ISEF rule. H0/H1 notation, variable tables and Gantt charts are coaching habits. They are not required, and I found no evidence about whether winners use them.

## The addendum must explain the changed methods without revealing results

The rules expect plans to change, and math projects most of all. ISEF: "If changes are made during the research before competing at an affiliated fair, such changes must be added as an addendum to the original plan... If there are no changes, an addendum is not required" ([ISEF Form 1A](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)). The Companion Guide describes the addendum as something "appended to the research plan to describe changes -- in methodology, materials, processes -- that occurred during the course of the project." It asks the Adult Sponsor to decide whether a departure is a "minor adjustment" or a "significant difference" that needs re-review ([Companion Guide p.13](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)). Your project is exempt and needed no pre-approval, so changing a statistical method does not require returning to an SRC before you continue. The SRC's final sign-off on Form 1B still certifies that "this project adheres to the approved Research Plan/Project Addendum" ([Rules Book p.33](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)). So every analysis on the poster has to appear in either the original plan or the addendum.

**The rules conflict here.** The ISEF Companion Guide says that when no further approvals are needed, "this addendum serves as a project summary to explain research that was conducted" ([Companion Guide p.10](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)). That wording invites a retrospective, results-flavored write-up. SSEF says the research plan "must not include data, conclusions, or unapproved procedures" ([2026-27 SSEF Rules Supplement](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ImzJHKi8CdMCD2b_YQWpJaAhyEbQcH74/view)), and SSEF's stricter rules take precedence in Florida. My reading is that the addendum is part of the "Research Plan/Project Addendum," so the SSEF ban covers it. Under that reading, the addendum explains *what was done differently and why, in methodological terms*. It does not say *what was found*. In practice, write "Added a robust-regression (Huber M-estimator) version of Solution 2 to test whether outlier sensitivity of ordinary least squares explains the original test's behavior." Do not write "Solution 2 false-alarmed in 100% of runs, so we tried RLM." The numbers and the verdict go in the abstract, the poster and the notebook. No source settles whether SSEF reviewers read the ban that strictly, so send one email to the Hillsborough fair (hillsboroughstemfair@gmail.com) or the SSEF SRC (flscifair.src@gmail.com) before January 11 to confirm.

**Your project has a timing advantage.** The repository's commit history shows checks that were pre-registered before they were run (for example, "Pre-register D7: robust-regression (RLM) check," followed by a separate "D7 results" commit). Those dated pre-registrations are exactly the evidence an addendum needs. Each addendum entry can carry the date the change was decided, and the timestamped pre-registration shows the method was fixed before its data were analyzed. That supports the rubric's "systematic data collection and analysis" item ([Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)). One check remains open. Form 1A and each member's Form 1B should carry signature dates from before experimentation began, and Hillsborough's Form 3 and the new Form 2A were both listed as "required before experimentation" ([HCPS 6-12 Timeline](https://www.hillsboroughschools.org/page/stem-fair-grades-6-12-timeline/)). If any of these were not signed in August or September, raise it with the adult sponsor now rather than at screening.

### Recommended addendum format

No official addendum template exists. The rules say only that it is "added" or "appended" to the original plan. The structure below is my recommendation, built from the elements the Companion Guide and the SSEF supplement name.

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Title block | "Research Plan Addendum" + project title + all team members' full names + date of addendum (MM/DD/YY). Place it after the unchanged original plan, in the same file |
| Statement of scope | One sentence: the original plan dated [August 2026] is unchanged above; this addendum records methodological changes made before the affiliated fair |
| One entry per change, in date order | (1) Date the change was decided; (2) section of the original plan it modifies, e.g. "c2 step 5" or "c4"; (3) what was originally planned; (4) what was done instead or added; (5) methodological reason, with no data values or conclusions; (6) which team member performs it, and any mentor role; (7) approval status, e.g. "exempt; no re-approval required per adult sponsor [name], MM/DD/YY" |
| Updated materials and sources | New software, datasets or references introduced by the changes, with full citations. These count toward the five-source minimum |
| AI disclosure update | Any new AI use since the original plan, cross-referenced to the Form 2A prompt log |

Two rules make the January deadlines strict. First, "after competing in an Affiliated Fair, projects may not be changed or amended," and "the approved research plan must include any and all phases of the project to be conducted." After that point the only permitted addition is more data collected "using the same methodology that was previously approved" ([Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)). Every method you plan to show at SSEF or ISEF therefore has to be in the addendum filed for Hillsborough. Second, team rules lock in at the same moment. A team has at most three members. It submits one Form 1A, one plan/addendum and one abstract, and each member signs a Form 1B. Once the project has competed at any level, "team membership cannot change" ([Rules Book pp.4-5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)).

## Generative AI must be disclosed in four places and kept out of the writing

The 2026-27 rule is direct: AI "may be used as a project resource but must be cited and given proper acknowledgment," and "a student may not use generative AI to write the research plan, abstract, poster or to create citations." Inappropriate AI use is an integrity violation that can make a project fail to qualify ([Rules Book p.3](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf); [Rule Modifications 2026-27](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/FINAL-Rule-Modifications-for-2026-27.pdf)). A Florida team discloses AI use in four places. The first three come from ISEF: item d of the plan (Acknowledgements) needs "a prompt log or an explanation" ([Form 1A](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)); the **new mandatory Form 2A** asks which project elements used AI and requests the AI Prompt Log, with personal identifying prompts removed ([Form 2A](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/2A-Student-Support-Disclosure-Form.pdf)); and any AI-made or AI-modified poster graphic must be cited with its creation date and tool ([Rules Book p.25](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)). The fourth is SSEF's rule that AI use "must be documented in the bibliography" ([SSEF Supplement](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ImzJHKi8CdMCD2b_YQWpJaAhyEbQcH74/view)).

The Society's Generative AI Use Table (October 2025, still current) separates allowed from banned uses in ways that matter for a statistics project ([Generative AI Use Table](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/Generative-AI-Use-Table.pdf)). AI-written code is allowed "only with explicit citation stating which portions of the code were AI generated and with a log of the prompts." Using AI to "help identify appropriate statistical tests or software tools" is allowed with a prompt log, but "interpretation of data must be done by the student researcher." Using AI to "initially write the research plan, abstract, paper or poster" is "never acceptable." Refinement after the student has written a complete draft is allowed with citation and a log. AI-produced conclusions, starter bibliographies and citations backing up claims are banned. If AI fixes bibliography formatting, every citation must still be verified by hand, and the Companion Guide "strongly advise[s]" against even that ([Companion Guide p.12](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)). The rule also points to a precise wording choice in your documents. If an AI assistant helped build simulation code, the addendum and Form 2A should name the specific scripts or functions involved. A general statement that "AI assisted with coding" does not meet the rule.

## Judges score the science the plan describes, not the plan document

ISEF judges score science projects out of **100 points**. Research Question is worth 10, Design and Methodology 15, Execution (data collection, analysis and interpretation) 20, Creativity and Potential Impact 20, and Presentation 35 (poster 10, interview 25). "Considerable emphasis is placed on two areas: Creativity and Presentation, especially in the Interview section" ([Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)). The official page has only science and engineering rubrics. **There is no mathematics rubric**, so a MATH project is almost certainly scored on the science rubric. That rubric suits your project well: the Execution criterion names "reproducibility of results" and "appropriate application of mathematical and statistical methods." The judging guidance also does not name the research plan or addendum as documents judges read. It names the poster (designed for "pre-interview evaluation"), "the student notebook," and Forms 1C and 7 ([Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)). The plan is a compliance document for the SRC. Judges grade the work it describes, so the poster and notebook must stay consistent with it.

| Criterion | Points | What it rewards in a test-auditing statistics project |
|---|---|---|
| Research Question | 10 | "clear and focused purpose," "identifies contribution to field of study," "testable" |
| Design and Methodology | 15 | "well designed plan," "variables and controls defined, appropriate and complete" |
| Execution | 20 | "systematic data collection and analysis," "reproducibility of results," "appropriate application of mathematical and statistical methods," "sufficient data" |
| Creativity & Potential Impact | 20 | creativity in any criterion; impact on "technology, economy, environment or society" |
| Poster | 10 | organization, clarity of graphics, "supporting documentation displayed" |
| Interview | 25 | understanding "interpretation and limitations," "degree of independence," "quality of ideas for further research," and "for team projects, contributions to and understanding of project by all members" |

All quoted criteria are from the [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/).

No ISEF document discusses null or negative results. The rubric rewards the quality of the design and the student's understanding, not whether the hypothesis was confirmed. A 2023 version of the guidelines tells judges to evaluate creativity by "research outcomes" that "offer different perspectives that open up new possibilities or new alternatives" ([Weber State 2023 ISEF Judging Guidelines](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/ritchey-science-fair/Analysis_Presentation/ISEF_Judging_Guidelines.pdf)). For a project showing that a published test (and a robust-regression fix for it) misbehaves, that points to one framing: present the result as a positive methodological finding about the test's error rate and the conditions behind it, not as "our fix did not work." The Society also tells affiliated-fair judges that "theoretical work" counts and that "the physical display is secondary to the student's knowledge of the subject" ([Judging at your Fair](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/judging-at-your-fair/)). The team-contribution item in the interview criterion has a practical consequence for the plan. ISEF only requires the plan to separate student steps from adult steps. Marking which team member runs which procedure, in the plan and addendum, gives each member a documented part to explain in the interview. That suggestion is my inference, not a rule.

## Winning abstracts are public; winning research plans are not

No ISEF Grand Award winner's research plan, project summary or addendum turned up in this search. These are SRC compliance documents and are almost never published. What is public is every finalist's 250-word abstract in the Society's searchable database (2014-2026, with category and "Only Winning Abstracts" filters) ([ISEF Projects Database](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/)). Board pages exist on isef.net. The closest thing to a public plan is a 2026 finalist's GitHub repository, where a regional-fair notebook has a "Research Plan:" cell written as a short bulleted task list. The README is organized like a paper (Overview, Dataset, Methodology, Key Results, Reproducibility, Future Work), not like Form 1A ([bradyzhou1 GitHub repo](https://github.com/bradyzhou1/Regeneron-ISEF-Finalist-Brain-Connectivity-in-Autism-with-ML)).

The abstracts show a consistent house style. ISEF requires a maximum of 250 words on one page, written after experimentation, describing only the students' own current-year work, with no acknowledgments, mentor or institution names, product names, figures or bibliography ([Rules Book p.29](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf); [How to Write an ISEF Abstract](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/how-to-write-an-isef-abstract/)). Recent quantitative MATH and BEHA abstracts follow the same arc: the problem and its stakes, the gap in existing methods, the aim, the named data and named methods, at least one quantitative result, and the implications, sometimes with a stated limitation. **MATH030 (2025)** modeled Swiss stock prices with Student-t random walks and reports kurtosis 9.34, fitted df 4.642, a Kolmogorov-Smirnov evaluation and a "70%" tail-modeling improvement ([MATH030](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=26951)). The **1st-place MATH028 (2025)** Prisoner's Dilemma abstract uses no numbers but stages its results in clear steps ([MATH028](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=27017)). **MATH040T (2025, 4th, a team project)** names its method with an acronym (SEWRA) and validates it "through simulations and case studies" ([MATH040T](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=27423)). **BEHA065 (2025, 4th)** states two hypotheses in the first person, names a difference-in-differences design and admits a "short post-reform period" limitation ([BEHA065](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=27461)). A keyword scan of the 2025 Grand Award titles found no finance-titled winners ([2025 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)). Market-data projects do appear among finalists, and statistics-method projects that win tend to present themselves as contributions to method. For your team, MATH040T is the closest model: a named statistical technique, tested by simulation, presented as improving how a field draws inferences.

## Conclusion

The rules treat the research plan as a pre-registration. A plan written before the work, followed by a dated record of every deviation, matches what the SRC checks and what judges reward under "systematic" and "reproducible" analysis. Your team already works this way: methods are pre-registered and timestamped before the results are run. The risk is not that the methods changed, which the Companion Guide expects for mathematics projects. The risk is an addendum written as a results summary that runs into SSEF's no-data rule, or an addendum filed after February 17, when the project is frozen.

The practical sequence follows from that. Keep the August plan untouched. Write a methods-only addendum with one dated entry per change, where each entry cites its pre-registration and names the team member responsible. Reconcile the AI disclosures across the plan, Form 2A and the bibliography. Confirm the addendum rule with the SSEF SRC. Put the full methodological story, including any 100% false-alarm finding, in the abstract, poster and notebook, where judges look and where findings are allowed.
