## Q1. Required sections of the Research Plan (exact headings and content); requirements for projects with no human, vertebrate, or hazardous elements

### Takeaway
The official 2026-2027 Form 1A instructions list five lettered parts, in this order:
- a. RATIONALE
- b. RESEARCH QUESTION(S), HYPOTHESIS(ES), ENGINEERING GOAL(S), EXPECTED OUTCOMES
- c. a detailed description with four bullets: List of materials, Procedures/Methodology, Risk and Safety, Data Analysis
- d. ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS (new; includes AI documentation)
- e. BIBLIOGRAPHY

Four subject-specific add-ons follow (human participants, vertebrate animals, PHBA, hazardous chemicals/activities/devices). None of them applies to a math/statistics project that uses only public financial data. Such a project still has to cover Risk and Safety and the source of its data. The Companion Guide says math and engineering plans may be "less detailed" at first, but if the work changes, a project summary/addendum explaining what was done is required.

### Cited Findings

**Exact text of the official instructions (Form 1A, page 32 of the 2026-2027 Rules Book)** — [1A PDF](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf); identical in [Rules Book p.32](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf):
- Page title: "Research Plan/Project Addendum"
- Banner: "A Research Plan/Project Addendum is required for ALL projects. The plan must be written BEFORE experimentation."
- "1. The Research Plan is to be written prior to experimentation."
- "2. If changes are made during the research before competing at an affiliated fair, such changes must be added as an addendum to the original plan. Be aware that some changes may require obtaining or returning to the IRB or SRC for appropriate review and approvals before conducting that portion of the project. If there are no changes, an addendum is not required."
- "3. The Research Plan/Project Addendum should include the following:"
  - "a. RATIONALE: Include a brief synopsis of the background that supports your research question/goal."
  - "b. RESEARCH QUESTION(S), HYPOTHESIS(ES), ENGINEERING GOAL(S), EXPECTED OUTCOMES: Include a brief synopsis of the background that supports your research question/goal." (The official form repeats item a's description here word for word. This looks like an editing error in the official document; see Inferences.)
  - "c. Describe the following in detail (include any required subject-specific additions to your Research Plan (listed below):"
    - "List of materials:"
    - "Procedures/Methodology: Detail all procedures and steps of the experimental design including methods for data collection, and when applicable, the source of data used. Be sure to differentiate steps to be taken by you and the steps that are to be taken by any supervising adult(s)."
    - "Risk and Safety: Identify any potential risks and describe safety precautions to be taken."
    - "Data Analysis: Describe the procedures you will use to analyze the data/results."
  - "d. ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS: List all individuals that supported your project and the contributions that they made. If AI was used, document this support with a prompt log or an explanation of the elements of the project that involved AI support."
  - "e. BIBLIOGRAPHY: List major references (e.g. science journal articles, books, internet sites) from your literature review. Ensure that all references are properly listed and verified (The use of AI for citations is discouraged; it is known to hallucinate and produce false citations which becomes an ethical issue if you cite sources that are false or that you did not actually reference.)"
- "Items 1-4 below are subject-specific guidelines for additional items to be included in your Research Plan/Project Addendum as applicable."
  1. "Human participants research": a. Participants, b. Recruitment, c. Methods, d. Risk Assessment, e. Protection of Privacy, f. Informed Consent Process.
  2. "Vertebrate animal research": a. alternatives and justification, b. potential impact, c. procedures and minimizing discomfort, d. animal numbers/species/strain/sex/age/source with justification, e. housing and daily care, f. disposition at end of study.
  3. "Potentially hazardous biological agents research": a. source of organism and BSL assessment/determination, b. safety precautions and disposal.
  4. "Hazardous chemicals, activities & devices": a. "Describe Risk Assessment process, supervision, safety precautions and specific methods of disposal." b. "Safety Data Sheets must be reviewed and should be referenced but do not need to be submitted with paperwork unless required by your fair."

**The rule text gives the elements in a slightly different form (Documentation and Approval #1, Rules Book p.5)** — [Rules Book](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf):
- "Student researchers must have a Research Plan that includes: a. Rationale b. Research Question/Hypothesis(es), Engineering Goal(s), Expected Outcomes c. List of Materials d. Procedures e. Risk and Safety f. Data Analysis g. Bibliography"
- This a-g list was edited for 2026-2027 and appears as a key change in the [Final Rule Modifications for 2026-27](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/FINAL-Rule-Modifications-for-2026-27.pdf).
- The a-g list has no Acknowledgements item. The Form 1A instructions do (item d).

**The Companion Guide (2026-2027) gives a slightly different, more explanatory version** — [Companion Guide pp.10-11](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf):
- Rationale: "Include a brief synopsis of the background that supports your research problem and explain why this research is important and if applicable, explain any societal impact of your research."
- Research Question(s)...: "How is this based on the rationale described above?"
- Procedures: "Detail all procedures and experimental design methods for data collection, and when applicable, the source of data used. Describe only your project. Do not include work done by mentor or others."
- Risk and Safety: "Identify any potential risks and safety precautions needed."
- Bibliography: "...If you plan to use vertebrate animals, one of these references must be an animal care reference."
- The Companion box still uses the label "Research Plan/Project Summary" and has no Acknowledgements item.

**Mentor delineation** — Rules Book p.5, Documentation and Approval #3: "If a mentor is to be involved in the project, the research plan should delineate what parts of the project are to be done by the student vs. those to be done by the mentor." [Rules Book](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)

**Math and public-data projects**
- Companion Guide p.10: "Some studies, such as an engineering design or mathematics projects, will be less detailed in the initial project plan and will change through the course of research. If such changes occur, a project summary that explains what was done is required and can be appended to the original research plan." — [Companion Guide](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- Companion Guide p.12 on risk for math: "some projects appear to have no risks at all. Mathematics comes to mind. However, the process of considering risks is important for all projects and not just to avoid papercuts or eye strain (the popular answer on a mathematics project risk assessment.)" It also says students should "consider the potential risks in their data management and to any psychological or privacy concerns their project may involve." — [Companion Guide](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- Exempt human-participant studies (no IRB pre-approval or human-participant paperwork), Rules Book p.8, #2: "Data/record review studies (e.g., baseball statistics, crime statistics) in which the data are taken from preexisting data sets that are publicly available and/or published and do not involve any interaction with humans or the collection of any data from a human participant for the purpose of the student's research project." — [Rules Book](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Form 1A item 10, "Source of Data", has checkboxes: "self-collected / mentor collected / publicly available / Other". — [1A PDF](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- Every project needs these forms (Rules Book p.5, #5): Checklist for Adult Sponsor (1), Student Checklist (1A), Research Plan/Project Addendum, Approval Form (1B), Student Support Disclosure Form (2A). — [Rules Book](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Pre-approval: the rules require IRB/SRC approval before experimentation for "Projects involving human participants, vertebrate animals, potentially hazardous biological agents and/or tissue" (Documentation #4). In addition, "ALL projects ... must be reviewed and approved by the SRC after experimentation and before competition in an Affiliated Fair" (Rules Book p.22). — [Rules Book](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a math/statistics project on public financial data, the applicable official structure is: a. Rationale; b. Research Question(s)/Hypothesis(es)/Engineering Goal(s)/Expected Outcomes; c. List of materials, Procedures/Methodology (including the data source), Risk and Safety, Data Analysis; d. Acknowledgements (with AI prompt log or explanation if AI was used); e. Bibliography. None of subject-specific items 1-4 apply.
- The official text of item b is a copy of item a's description, so it gives no real guidance for b. The Companion Guide's wording ("How is this based on the rationale described above?") is the best official statement of what b should contain.
- The official sources disagree on whether Acknowledgements is part of the plan. The Rules body (a-g) and the Companion Guide leave it out; the 2026-2027 Form 1A instructions include it as item d. A plan that includes item d satisfies both versions.
- Public financial market data (prices, indices) is not about identifiable human participants, so it falls under the exempt data/record review category. No IRB pre-approval or Form 4 is needed, but the SRC still reviews the project after experimentation.

### Gaps
- No official statement explains why the Form 1A item b description duplicates item a. It is presumably an editing error, but this is not confirmed.
- No official statement on how detailed "Risk and Safety" must be for a computational/statistics project, beyond the Companion Guide's comments on data management and privacy.

## Q2. Length limit, required header information, and format (fixed form or free format)

### Takeaway
The official instructions set no length limit and require no header block. Identifying information (student/team names, title, school, adult sponsor, dates, data source) goes on the fixed Student Checklist (1A) form. The Research Plan/Project Addendum itself is a free-format document attached to Form 1A that follows the listed headings. Affiliated fairs may supply templates or online systems.

### Cited Findings
- The Form 1A page ends: "Provide a complete Research Plan/Project Addendum with this form." The 1A fields are: 1 Student/Team Leader (grade, email, phone) and Team Members; 2 Title of Project; 3 School; 4 Adult Sponsor; 5 where experimentation will be conducted; 6 non-home/non-school work sites; 7 whether SRC/IRB/IACUC pre-approval is needed and the tentative start date; 8 actual start and end dates of this year's experimentation/data collection; 9 continuation status; 10 Source of Data. — [1A PDF](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- Form 1A header: "This form is required for ALL projects. This form must be completed by the student(s) BEFORE experimentation." — [1A PDF](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- The Forms overview page describes item #10 of Form 1A: "Attach a Research Plan and Project Summary, as outlined in the Research Plan and Summary Instructions, which describes the project in detail". This page still uses the older "Summary" wording. — [Overview of Forms and Dates](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/overview-of-forms-and-dates/)
- Templates: "Student researchers can be aided in the development of their research plan using a template and/or an online system that is used by an affiliated fair ... If your school or affiliated fair does not use an online system to capture this information, there is a research plan template on the Society for Science website." Also: "A student should consult with their Adult Sponsor or Qualified Scientist about the best template to use as they begin." — [Companion Guide pp.12-13](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- Companion Guide: "A research plan does not need to have all of the answers; but it should be a clear roadmap for the student researcher to share the intended steps and to review and receive feedback before beginning." — [Companion Guide p.10](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- Digital forms: "Submission of forms generated by a digital system are allowable under the following conditions: a. The forms must have the same content and order as ISEF forms." Paperwork submitted to the Society for ISEF must be scanned and submitted through the online portal. — [Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- The ISEF Rules FAQ has no entries on research plan length, format or template. — [ISEF Rules FAQ](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/)

### Inferences
- Because the instructions say the plan "should include the following" with lettered headings, the safest format uses those exact headings in that order (a-e, with c's four bullets as subheadings), plus a top line repeating the 1A title and names for identification. The header line is good practice, not an official requirement.
- The "same content and order as ISEF forms" condition applies to digitally generated forms. It is a reason to keep the official heading order.

### Gaps
- I did not locate the Society for Science research plan template that the Companion Guide mentions. Its URL and whether it sets length or header expectations are unverified.
- No official length limit or recommended length was found. Affiliated (regional/state) fairs may set their own; check the specific fair.

## Q3. Research Plan Addendum rules (changes after SRC approval or after experimentation starts), writing the plan first, and team projects

### Takeaway
The plan must be written before experimentation. Changes made during the research, before competing at an affiliated fair, are added as an addendum appended to the original plan. If the change needs new approval (or the project had IRB/SRC pre-approval), it must be re-approved before data collection resumes. No addendum is needed if nothing changed. After competing at an affiliated fair, the project may not be changed or amended. Teams jointly submit one 1A, one Research Plan/Project Addendum and one abstract; each member signs their own Form 1B.

### Cited Findings
**Writing before experimentation**
- "A Research Plan/Project Addendum is required for ALL projects. The plan must be written BEFORE experimentation." and "1. The Research Plan is to be written prior to experimentation." — [1A PDF p.32](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- "This research plan should be completed before experimentation and will inform the forms required for your research." (Documentation and Approval #2) — [Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- "The forms should be filled out and signed before any research takes place. (Only Forms 1C, 5B, 7, and the abstract are done after the research.)" — [Overview of Forms and Dates](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/overview-of-forms-and-dates/)
- FAQ on start dates: the "literature review and the design of your study will occur prior to your start date." — [ISEF Rules FAQ](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/faq/)

**Addendum: what, when, format**
- "2. If changes are made during the research before competing at an affiliated fair, such changes must be added as an addendum to the original plan. Be aware that some changes may require obtaining or returning to the IRB or SRC for appropriate review and approvals before conducting that portion of the project. If there are no changes, an addendum is not required." — [1A PDF p.32](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- "Any changes to the original research plan should be addressed in a research plan addendum." (Documentation and Approval #3) — [Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- "7. After initial IRB/SRC approval (if required), any proposed changes in the Student Checklist (1A) and Research Plan/ Project Summary must be re-approved before laboratory experimentation/data collection resumes." — [Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- "8. After competing in an Affiliated Fair, projects may not be changed or amended. The approved research plan must include any and all phases of the project to be conducted. Additional data may be collected using the same methodology that was previously approved for the affiliated fair." — [Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Human participants: "After initial IRB approval, a student with any proposed changes in the Research Plan must repeat the approval process and regain approval before resuming interaction (recruitment, data collection) with human participants." — [Rules Book p.8](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Companion Guide, "PROJECT ADDENDUM": "The International Rules labels the research plan the 'Research Plan / Project Addendum' to address modifications or adjustments that were made to the project after it began. A project addendum would be appended to the research plan to describe changes -- in methodology, materials, processes -- that occurred during the course of the project. This is especially useful for an engineering design project that is iterative." — [Companion Guide p.13](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- Companion Guide, "EDITS/CORRECTIONS AFTER THE INITIAL REVIEW": the Adult Sponsor should judge whether a departure from the plan is a "minor adjustment" or "a significant difference that either now requires pre-approval to proceed or needs to return to the SRC or IRB that initially reviewed the project ... This change should be reflected in an Addendum to the initial Research Plan." — [Companion Guide p.13](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- Companion Guide, requirements box: "If no additional approvals are required, this addendum serves as a project summary to explain research that was conducted." and "If no changes are made from the original research plan, no project summary is required." — [Companion Guide p.10](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- SRC sign-off: Form 1B §3, "Final ISEF Affiliated Fair SRC Approval (Required for ALL Projects) ... I certify that this project adheres to the approved Research Plan/Project Addendum and complies with all ISEF Rules." Form 1B §2a (only for projects needing prior approval): "My signature indicates approval of the Research Plan/Project Addendum before the student begins experimentation." — [Rules Book p.33](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Continuations: Form 1A item 9a requires attaching "the previous year's Abstract and Research Plan/Project Addendum" plus Form 7. — [1A PDF](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- Research window: research is judged only on "laboratory experiment/data collection performed over 12 continuous months beginning no earlier than January 2026 and ending May 2027". Eligibility #4: "no more than 12 months of continuous research and may not include research performed before January 2026." — [Rules Book pp.3-4](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)

**Team projects**
- "6. Each team member must submit an Approval Form (1B). Team members must jointly submit the Checklist for Adult Sponsor (1), one abstract, a Student Checklist (1A), a Research Plan/Project Addendum and other required forms." and "7. Full names of all team members must appear on the abstract and forms." — [Rules Book pp.4-5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- "Teams must have no more than three members." Team membership cannot change during a research year unless the local SRC approves the change for extenuating circumstances. "Once a project has competed in a science fair at any level, team membership cannot change and the project cannot be converted from an individual project to a team project or vice versa." — [Rules Book p.4](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- The Form 2A in the official Book.pdf says: "Team members should complete one form for the project." — [Rules Book p.34](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf). An earlier copy of the 2026-2027 rules hosted by a local fair ([trine.edu mirror](https://www.trine.edu/science-fair/ISEF%20Rules%202026-2027.pdf)) lacks this sentence, so the official PDF has been revised since first release.

### Inferences
- In practice the addendum is a dated section appended to the original plan, not a rewrite. It describes what changed (methodology, materials, processes, data, analysis) and why. If the change needs approval, the approval must come before that part of the work is done.
- For an exempt project (no prior SRC/IRB approval needed), changes still go into the addendum. The SRC's final review checks that the project "adheres to the approved Research Plan/Project Addendum".
- Any analysis planned for after the affiliated fair has to be written into the approved plan beforehand ("must include any and all phases"). After that fair, only more data collected with the same approved methodology is allowed.

### Gaps
- No official addendum template or required format (headings, dating, signatures) was found. The rules say only that it is "added" or "appended" to the original plan.

## Q4. Abstract (Official Abstract and Certification) requirements

### Takeaway
The abstract is a maximum 250-word, one-page abstract written after experimentation. It summarizes only the current year's work, done by the student(s). The recommended content is purpose, procedure/methodology, key results, and conclusions/applications. It must not include acknowledgments (mentor or institution names), self-promotion or external endorsements, logos or commercial product names, or work done by the mentor. Generative AI may not write it. At ISEF it is entered in the online Finalist Questionnaire portal, checked for plagiarism, and approved by the ISEF SRC.

### Cited Findings
- "After experimentation, each student or team must submit a (maximum) 250-word, one-page abstract which summarizes the current year's work. The abstract must describe research conducted by the student(s), not by the supervising adult(s)." (Documentation and Approval #11) — [Rules Book p.5](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Rules Book p.29, "Information on Required Abstract & Certification":
  - "After finishing research and experimentation, you are required to write a (maximum) 250 word, one-page abstract. For ISEF, this abstract is written in the online Finalist Questionnaire portal and submitted electronically. This abstract must be written in your own words and will be run through a plagiarism checker."
  - "It is recommended that it include the following: a. purpose of the experiment b. procedure/methodology used c. most important/significant results you found d. conclusions/research applications"
  - "Only minimal reference to previous work may be included."
  - "An abstract must not include the following: a. acknowledgments (including naming the research institution and/or mentor with which you were working), or self-promotions and external endorsements b. logos or proper names of commercial products c. work or procedures done by the mentor"
  - Certification question 2: "This abstract describes only procedures performed by me/us, reflects my/our own independent research, and represents one year's work only."
  - Revisions are permitted in the portal "through late April". Once approved, two copies with a gold embossed seal are provided, and "No other format or version of your approved Abstract will be allowed for any purpose at the ISEF."
  - "This form may not be relevant for your regional or state fair; please refer to instructions from your affiliated fair."
  — [Rules Book p.29](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Continuations: "The display board and abstract must reflect the current year's work only." — [Rules Book p.4](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Language: "Student project boards and abstracts must be in English." — [Rules Book p.3](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- The Society's abstract web page says: "The abstract should be 250 words or less." Its sections are Purpose, Procedure, Observations/Data/Results, and Conclusions ("confined to a short summary in 1-2 sentences"). Other points from the page:
  - "Do not include acknowledgements in the abstract."
  - "Do not include ... tables, charts, graphs or other images."
  - "An abstract should only include procedures done by the Finalist."
  - "The Regeneron ISEF abstract does not include a bibliography."
  - For continuations, "summarize the current year's work only."
  — [How to Write a Regeneron ISEF Abstract](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/how-to-write-an-isef-abstract/)
- The regional/local PDF version of the official abstract form is at [21-Categories.pdf](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Resources/Abstracts/21-Categories.pdf), linked from [Overview of Forms and Dates](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/overview-of-forms-and-dates/). The file name says "21 categories", but the 2026-2027 rules list 22 categories.

### Inferences
- The rules book and the web page agree on the 250-word maximum and the four content elements. The web page adds "no tables/charts/images" and "no bibliography".

### Gaps
- I did not check whether the regional PDF abstract form (21-Categories.pdf) has been updated for 22 categories.

## Q5. Rules on disclosing AI / generative AI use (2025-2027)

### Takeaway
For 2026-2027, AI may be used as a cited and acknowledged resource. Generative AI may NOT write the research plan, abstract or poster, or create citations. Inappropriate AI use is an ethics violation that can make a project fail to qualify. Disclosure happens in three places:
- the Research Plan's Acknowledgements section (prompt log or explanation)
- the new mandatory Form 2A, which asks whether generative AI was used, which elements it was used in, and for the AI Prompt Log
- AI-created or AI-modified graphics, which must be cited with the creation date and the tool used

### Cited Findings
- Eligibility/Limitations #8, a key change for 2026-27: "Artificial Intelligence (AI) may be used as a project resource but must be cited and given proper acknowledgment. A student is expected to do independent work and all materials presented must be in the researcher's own words. A student may not use generative AI to write the research plan, abstract, poster or to create citations (it is known to hallucinate and falsify references)." — [Rules Book p.3](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf); [Rule Modifications 2026-27](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/FINAL-Rule-Modifications-for-2026-27.pdf)
- Ethics Statement, Integrity: "The presentation of fraudulent data, the evidence of plagiarism or the inappropriate use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) are prohibited and grounds for the project to fail to qualify." — [Rules Book p.3](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Research Plan item d: "If AI was used, document this support with a prompt log or an explanation of the elements of the project that involved AI support." Item e: "The use of AI for citations is discouraged..." — [1A PDF p.32](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/Apps/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- Form 2A (Student Support Disclosure), "This form is required for ALL projects. This form may be completed before or after experimentation." Its AI Usage Disclosure section reads: "Did your project involve the use of Generative AI?" If yes, "provide an attachment with Generative AI used in your project and in what elements of your project it was used." and "Please provide the AI Prompt Log containing the prompts and responses from the Generative AI or AI Wrapper used. Be sure to remove any personal prompts which contain personal identifying information about yourself or others. If you do not have access to your prompt log, then please provide a summary of the prompts and responses involved in your project." The form also requires disclosure of paid support. — [Rules Book p.34](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf); standalone form: [2A PDF](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Forms/2A-Student-Support-Disclosure-Form.pdf)
- Form 2A "is a new form that is required for all projects" (Rule Modifications 2026-27). The Companion Guide says: "New in 2027, all students must now complete the Student Support Disclosure Form 2A ... how artificial intelligence was used in the project." — [Rule Modifications](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/FINAL-Rule-Modifications-for-2026-27.pdf); [Companion Guide p.6](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- Display rules: "The use of AI to create graphics is acceptable but these graphics must be cited as AI created. AI citations must include the date that a graphic was created and the AI used to create the graphic." and "If using AI to modify any graphic, a citation for the original graphic must be provided ... The citation must also include the modification date and AI used." — [Rules Book p.25](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Book.pdf)
- Generative AI Use Table (Society for Science, Oct 2025; also reproduced in the Companion Guide pp.5-6). It is "a preliminary model; Affiliated fairs may adopt their own, more strict, policies." Rows relevant here:
  - Identify or summarize key points of an article before you read it: Yes, "Acceptable without explicit citation".
  - Summarize a book or article and reproduce the summary without reading the source: No.
  - AI chatbot to generate and develop ideas: Yes, "may require explicit citation depending on circumstances". The table says to keep a prompt log; the Companion Guide says "Strongly encouraged to keep a log".
  - "Use generative AI to initially write the research plan, abstract, paper or poster": No, "Never acceptable. This must be the independent work of the student. Guidance or refinement after the initial document has been completed can be done with explicit citation and a log."
  - You write the abstract and AI sharpens only grammar and syntax: Yes, without explicit citation.
  - "Use AI to write initial code for your project": Yes, "only with explicit citation stating which portions of the code were AI generated and with a log of the prompts."
  - AI-produced flowchart, graphic or image: Yes, clearly marked as AI-generated with explicit citation of how it was made.
  - AI adds points to your research paper: No.
  - AI produces conclusions or future steps: No.
  - "Use AI to help identify appropriate statistical tests or software tools. (Interpretation of data must be done by the student researcher)": Yes, "requires a log of your prompts as part of your research notebook."
  - AI collects data and writes the research plan, or provides citations that back up claims: No.
  - AI produces a starter bibliography: No.
  - AI fixes bibliography structure or formatting: Yes, without citation, but "You must review and verify all citations as valid."
  — [Generative AI Use Table](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/Generative-AI-Use-Table.pdf); [Companion Guide](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)
- The Companion Guide adds: "It is strongly advised not to use AI tools for formatting references, however if it is used, all references must be verified manually by the student researcher." Also: "Having citations that are falsified by any means is a serious ethical concern and grounds for a project to fail to qualify." — [Companion Guide pp.5, 12](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/Rules-Companion-Guide.pdf)

### Inferences
- The AI Use Table and the Companion Guide differ slightly on bibliography formatting. The table marks AI formatting of a bibliography acceptable with verification; the Companion Guide's prose "strongly advise[s]" against it. Either way, every citation must be checked by hand.
- For a statistics project that used AI coding help, the safe path covers four things: state which portions of the code were AI-generated; keep the full prompt log; attach the log or a summary to Form 2A and reference it in the plan's Acknowledgements; write all interpretation and conclusions without AI.

### Gaps
- The Society's 2027 FAQ points to the AI table for guidance and has no AI-specific FAQ entries beyond that. The AI Use Table is dated Oct 2025 (filed under the 2026 folder), and I found no 2027-specific revision of it.
