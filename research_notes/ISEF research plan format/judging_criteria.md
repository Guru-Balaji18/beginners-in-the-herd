# How Regeneron ISEF Projects Are Judged (criteria, written materials, null results, AI)

Research date: 2026-11-20. Source years are given for each item. "Fetched" means the page itself was read. "Search snippet" means the claim comes only from a search-engine summary and was not read on the page; treat those as weaker.

## Q1. What are the official ISEF judging criteria and weights for science projects, and do math projects use a different rubric?

### Takeaway
The current official Regeneron ISEF Grand Award criteria give science projects 100 points: Research Question 10, Design and Methodology 15, Execution: Data Collection, Analysis and Interpretation 20, Creativity & Potential Impact 20, and Presentation 35 (Poster 10, Interview 25). Engineering projects use a separate rubric. There is **no separate mathematics rubric** at ISEF: the official page has only the science and engineering versions.

### Cited Findings
**Official page (societyforscience.org, fetched; page footer "© 2026", no revision date shown)**
- Intro, verbatim: "The following evaluation criteria are used for judging at the Regeneron ISEF." Science and engineering "have different criteria, each with five sections" "as well as suggested scoring for each section." "Each section includes key items to consider for evaluation both before and after the interview." — [Society for Science, Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- Science criteria, verbatim bullets — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/):
  - **1. Research Question (10 pts):** "clear and focused purpose"; "identifies contribution to field of study"; "testable using scientific methods"
  - **2. Design and Methodology (15 pts):** "well designed plan and data collection methods"; "variables and controls defined, appropriate and complete"
  - **3. Execution: Data Collection, Analysis and Interpretation (20 pts):** "systematic data collection and analysis"; "reproducibility of results"; "appropriate application of mathematical and statistical methods"; "sufficient data collected to support interpretation and conclusions"
  - **4. Creativity & Potential Impact (20 pts):** "project demonstrates significant creativity in one or more of the above criteria"; "project has impact or potential impact in its field and/or in technology, economy, environment or society"
  - **5. Presentation (35 pts)**
    - Poster (10 pts): "logical organization of material"; "clarity of graphics and legends"; "supporting documentation displayed"
    - Interview (25 pts): "clear, concise, thoughtful responses to questions"; "understanding of basic science relevant to project"; "understanding interpretation and limitations of results and conclusions"; "degree of independence in conducting project"; "recognition of potential impact in science, society and/or economics"; "quality of ideas for further research"; "for team projects, contributions to and understanding of project by all members"
- Emphasis, verbatim: "Considerable emphasis is placed on two areas: Creativity and Presentation, especially in the Interview section." — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- Engineering rubric (for contrast; same weights): Research Problem 10 (practical need, criteria for solution, constraints); Design and Methodology 15 (alternatives explored, solution identified, prototype/model developed); Execution: Construction and Testing 20 (prototype matches design, tested in multiple conditions/trials, engineering skill and completeness); Creativity & Potential Impact 20; Presentation 35 (same poster and interview items as science). — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/) (fetched, paraphrased)

**Earlier version (2023) with fuller explanatory text**
- The 2023 guidelines, as reproduced by a fair, call criterion 4 just "Creativity (20 pts)" with only the creativity bullet. The current page names it "Creativity & Potential Impact" and adds the impact bullet, so the impact item looks like a post-2023 addition. — [Weber State / Ritchey Science & Engineering Fair, "Regeneron ISEF Judging Guidelines—2023"](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/ritchey-science-fair/Analysis_Presentation/ISEF_Judging_Guidelines.pdf) (fetched PDF)
- The same 2023 document says the list "was extensively reviewed and revised by the Regeneron ISEF Judge Advisory Committee". It defines Creativity as: "A creative project demonstrates imagination and inventiveness. Such projects often offer different perspectives that open up new possibilities or new alternatives. Judges should place emphasis on research outcomes in evaluating creativity." — [Weber State 2023 ISEF Judging Guidelines](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/ritchey-science-fair/Analysis_Presentation/ISEF_Judging_Guidelines.pdf)
- It defines the interview as: "The interview provides the opportunity to interact with the students and evaluate their understanding of the project's basic science, interpretation and limitations of the results and conclusions." — [Weber State 2023 ISEF Judging Guidelines](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/ritchey-science-fair/Analysis_Presentation/ISEF_Judging_Guidelines.pdf)

**Math and computational projects**
- The official criteria page contains only "Science" and "Engineering" rubrics. Nothing on it addresses mathematics or computational projects. — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/) (fetched)
- The ISEF categories page lists Mathematics (MATH), Computational Biology and Bioinformatics (CBIO) and Software Design (SFTD) as categories. It says "your judges will be assigned based on their expertise in your chosen subcategory." It does not say which rubric applies to which category. — [Society for Science, ISEF Categories and Subcategories](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/categories-and-subcategories/) (fetched)
- At least one university-run fair judging sheet says that for engineering projects "or some projects in categories such as computer science and mathematical sciences", the questions in its engineering section are more appropriate. This is a local fair's practice, not ISEF policy. — [University of Notre Dame Science Fair, Judging Criteria](https://sciencefair.nd.edu/exhibitor-resources/judging-criteria/) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- A statistics or methodology project entered in MATH (or another science category) will almost certainly be scored on the **science** rubric. The "appropriate application of mathematical and statistical methods" and "reproducibility of results" items in Execution (20 pts) map directly onto a project that audits a published statistical test.
- 45 of the 100 points (Research Question 10, Design 15, Execution 20) are about the logic of the investigation. Most of what the research plan describes (question, design, controls, analysis plan) feeds these three criteria, so the plan is effectively the blueprint judges grade against.
- Because Creativity is "judged on research outcomes," showing that an accepted, published test is flawed fits the "different perspectives that open up new possibilities" language well.

### Gaps
- No Society for Science page gives an official mathematics-specific rubric (proof rigor, originality of a theorem, and so on). I found none.
- The official page has no revision date. The "© 2026" footer shows only that the page was live in 2026. I found no separate 2027 criteria document.
- No Florida SSEF or Hillsborough-specific rubric was retrieved. A St. Johns County (FL) secondary rubric and 2026–27 Research Plan Directions exist ([SJCSD Secondary Judging Rubric](https://www.stjohns.k12.fl.us/stem-fair/wp-content/uploads/sites/182/2024/11/SJCSD-Secondary-Judging-Rubric.pdf); [SJCSD Research Plan Directions 2026-2027](https://www.stjohns.k12.fl.us/stem-fair/wp-content/uploads/sites/182/2026/06/Research-Plan-Directions-2026-2027.pdf)), but I did not read them.

## Q2. How do judges use the research plan, project summary/abstract and Research Plan Addendum? Do they review them before interviews?

### Takeaway
Official judging guidance explicitly points judges to the **poster** (designed for "pre-interview evaluation"), the **student notebook**, the **abstract** (for continuation projects) and special forms (**1C** and **7**). It does **not** say that judges read the research plan, project summary or Research Plan Addendum. Those are rules and Scientific Review Committee (SRC) compliance documents. In practice judges grade the science that the plan describes, but the plan document itself is not formally named as a judged item.

### Cited Findings
- Posters, verbatim: "Students are encouraged to design their posters in a clear and informative manner to allow pre-interview evaluation and to enable the interview to become an in-depth discussion." — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- Notebook and forms, verbatim: "Judges should examine the student notebook and, if present, any special forms such as Form 1C (Regulated Research Institution/Industrial Setting) and Form 7 (Continuation of Projects)." — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- For multi-year projects, the interview covers only the current year's work, and judges review the abstract and Form 7 to confirm this year's progress. — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/) (fetched, paraphrased)
- Independence: for work at a research or industrial facility, judges assess independence using Form 1C. For work at home or school, they check whether the student received mentoring. — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/); [Weber State 2023 ISEF Judging Guidelines](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/ritchey-science-fair/Analysis_Presentation/ISEF_Judging_Guidelines.pdf)
- Required Research Plan components (2026–27 rules): "Rationale," "Research Question/Hypothesis(es), Engineering Goal(s), Expected Outcomes," "List of Materials," "Procedures," "Risk and Safety," "Data Analysis," and "Bibliography". Also: "This research plan should be completed before experimentation." — [Society for Science, Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/) (fetched; the research window "January 2026 … May 2027" marks this as the 2026–27 rules)
- Addendum, verbatim: "Any changes to the original research plan should be addressed in a research plan addendum." Proposed changes to the Student Checklist (1A) and "Research Plan/ Project Summary must be re-approved" before data collection resumes. "After competing in an Affiliated Fair, projects may not be changed or amended." The forms list includes a "Research Plan/Project Addendum." — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- Mentored projects: the plan should "delineate what parts of the project were done by the student." — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- Abstract: "a (maximum) 250-word, one-page abstract" submitted after experimentation. It "must describe research conducted by the student(s), not by the supervising adult(s)." "The display board and abstract must reflect the current year's work only." — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- A data book and research paper are "strongly recommended for judging purposes" but not required. — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- Form 7 "must be displayed at the project booth" (continuation projects). — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- Society guidance for affiliated-fair judges: "The physical display is secondary to the student's knowledge of the subject." "ONLY research conducted within the current year is to be evaluated." Judges should "Look for evidence of laboratory, field or theoretical work, not just library research or gadgeteering." The page says nothing about research plans or abstracts. — [Society for Science, Judging at your Fair](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/judging-at-your-fair/) (fetched; © 2026)
- Fair standard: "A science fair should have a minimum of two judges reviewing any project per round of judging." "The decisions of judges are final." — [Society for Science, Standards for Science Fairs: Judging](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/standards-for-science-fairs/judging/) (fetched)
- One regional fair document says judges "will have access to these materials before judging, but there is no guarantee that they will review them". A research paper is optional, and judges are not obligated to review one. — [AzSEF 2025 Senior Division Project Requirements](https://azscience.org/media/g43cvdmg/azsef25-senior-division-project-requirements.pdf) (search snippet; regional, not ISEF)
- Judging day at ISEF 2025 was Monday, May 12, 2025 (Columbus, OH), with finalists at their projects for interviews during the day. — [Society for Science, Regeneron ISEF 2025](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/regeneron-isef-2025/) (search snippet)
- One university fair's judge guide suggests about 8–10 minutes per student interview, with one judge at a project at a time (local practice, not ISEF). — [DRSEF Judge Guide & Scoresheets (SMU)](https://www.smu.edu/-/media/site/lyle/caruth-institute/drsef/generalinformation/drsef_judge_guide_scoresheets.pdf) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- Treat the **poster and abstract** as the documents judges will actually read before and during the interview. Treat the **research plan and addendum** as documents that must be correct and consistent (the SRC checks them, and judges may see them in the paperwork). If the poster contradicts the plan, a judge who does look will notice.
- The addendum is the official place to record changes such as dropped or added analyses. Documenting why a planned test was replaced protects the project and supports the "systematic" and "independence" criteria.
- A clear, labeled notebook or data log is explicitly named for judges, so it is a higher-value judging document than the research plan.

### Gaps
- I found no official Society statement on whether ISEF Grand Award judges get the research plan, the project summary or the addendum in advance (for example, through an online portal before judging day). This would need the confidential judge handbook or direct confirmation (judging@societyforscience.org, per a [virology.ws post](https://virology.ws/?p=12699), search snippet).
- The rules page does not define "Project Summary" separately from the Research Plan. It appears only in the phrase "Research Plan/ Project Summary". — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- The official interview length and number of judges per finalist at ISEF were not found. A 2025 IEEE judge's account exists ([IEEE Computer Society, "Empowering the Next Generation of Innovators: My Experience as a Grand Award Judge at ISEF 2025"](https://www.computer.org/publications/tech-news/insider-membership-news/empowering-the-next-generation-innovators)), but the site returned 403/SSL errors and could not be read.

## Q3. What do experienced judges and Society for Science say about strong vs weak projects, and about AI tools?

### Takeaway
The official criteria reward a focused, testable question, a design with "defined, appropriate and complete" variables and controls, systematic and reproducible analysis with appropriate statistics, enough data for the conclusions, and above all an interview that shows real understanding of the "interpretation and limitations" of the results and the student's independence. Generative AI is allowed only in narrow, logged and cited ways. It may **never** write the first draft of the research plan, abstract or poster, produce conclusions, or generate citations.

### Cited Findings
- Strong-project features in the official text: "clear and focused purpose", "testable using scientific methods", "variables and controls defined, appropriate and complete", "reproducibility of results", "appropriate application of mathematical and statistical methods", "sufficient data collected to support interpretation and conclusions", "understanding interpretation and limitations of results and conclusions", "degree of independence", "quality of ideas for further research". — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- Society guidance to judges: "The physical display is secondary to the student's knowledge of the subject." Judges should seek "evidence of laboratory, field or theoretical work, not just library research or gadgeteering". "Theoretical work" explicitly counts, which matters for math and simulation projects. — [Judging at your Fair](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/judging-at-your-fair/)
- Judges should use "an encouraging tone" and "should not criticize, treat lightly, or display boredom toward projects they personally consider unimportant." — [Judging at your Fair](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/judging-at-your-fair/)
- Physics Forums Insights: judges look for "simple and clear connections between the articulated hypothesis" and how results are presented, and "will quickly spot errors in discussions and draw conclusions that are not well-founded". — [Physics Forums Insights, "Learn the Secrets of Successful Science Projects"](https://www.physicsforums.com/insights/secrets-successful-science-projects/) (search snippet; author a long-time judge per the site, date not verified)
- **AI rules (2026–27 Rules for All Projects):** "Artificial Intelligence (AI) may be used as a project resource but must be cited and given proper acknowledgment." "A student may not use generative AI to write the research plan, abstract, poster or to create citations." Inappropriate AI use is listed under integrity violations. — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- **Generative AI Use Table (Society for Science and the Regeneron ISEF SRC, dated "Oct 2025"; read in full)** — [Generative AI Use Table (PDF)](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/Generative-AI-Use-Table.pdf):
  - "Use generative AI to initially write the research plan, abstract, paper or poster": No, "Never acceptable. This must be the independent work of the student. Guidance or refinement after the initial document has been completed can be done with explicit citation and a log."
  - You write the abstract and AI sharpens the language: Yes, "without explicit citation only if changes suggested by AI are limited to grammar and syntax".
  - "Use AI to write initial code for your project": Yes, "only with explicit citation stating which portions of the code were AI generated and with a log of the prompts."
  - "Use AI to help identify appropriate statistical tests or software tools. (Interpretation of data must be done by the student researcher)": Yes, "requires a log of your prompts as part of your research notebook."
  - "Use AI to produce your conclusions, future steps, etc.": No, never acceptable.
  - Starter bibliography from AI: No. Fixing bibliography formatting: Yes, but "You must review and verify all citations as valid."
  - AI-generated flowcharts or images must be "clearly marked as AI-generated" with citation.
  - AI chatbot to help generate ideas: acceptable, "may require explicit citation"; "Maintain a logbook of your prompts as part of your research notebook."
  - "This is a preliminary model; Affiliated fairs may adopt their own, more strict, policies."
- The 2026–27 rule modifications carry the same AI-citation wording forward. — [FINAL Rule Modifications for 2026-27 (PDF)](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2027/Rules/FINAL-Rule-Modifications-for-2026-27.pdf) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- For a computational or statistics project that uses AI coding assistants: keep a prompt log in the notebook, label which code is AI-generated, and cite it. The research plan, abstract, poster text and conclusions must be written by the student first. Using AI to suggest statistical tests is allowed with a log, but interpreting the results must be the student's own work.
- "Degree of independence" and "understanding interpretation and limitations" make up a large share of the 25 interview points. A student who can explain exactly why a test fails (assumptions, simulation design, error rates) without notes will score well there.

### Gaps
- I found no firsthand Society for Science "tips from judges" article that I could read in full. The Science Buddies judge-perspective page returned 403.
- I found no official ISEF statement on pre-registration, robustness checks or reproducibility packages (code sharing). "Reproducibility of results" is the only official hook.

## Q4. How do ISEF judges view null or negative results, and how should a methodological finding (showing a published test is flawed) be presented?

### Takeaway
No official ISEF document addresses null results directly. The rubric scores the quality of the question, the design, the analysis and the student's understanding of "interpretation and limitations," not whether a hypothesis was confirmed. Science-fair guidance consistently says that well-executed unsupported hypotheses are judged as fairly as supported ones. I found no ISEF guidance specific to "methodological critique" projects.

### Cited Findings
- None of the rubric items reward confirming the hypothesis. The Execution and Interview items reward "systematic data collection and analysis", "sufficient data collected to support interpretation and conclusions" and "understanding interpretation and limitations of results and conclusions." — [Grand Award Judging Criteria](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/grand-award/criteria/)
- Creativity "demonstrates imagination and inventiveness… often offer[s] different perspectives that open up new possibilities or new alternatives. Judges should place emphasis on research outcomes in evaluating creativity." — [Weber State 2023 ISEF Judging Guidelines](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/ritchey-science-fair/Analysis_Presentation/ISEF_Judging_Guidelines.pdf)
- Science Buddies says judges do not care whether the hypothesis is proved or disproved; they care how much the student learned. It also says judges can be equally impressed by projects that start with a faulty hypothesis, with weight on whether the experiment was well controlled. — [Science Buddies, "Hypothesis Trouble: What to do when a science project fails"](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/blog/hypothesis-trouble-failed-science-project) (search snippet; the page returned 403 when fetched)
- Physics Forums Insights says projects with unsupported, disproven or partially supported hypotheses "can be just as successful" as strongly supported ones. — [Physics Forums Insights](https://www.physicsforums.com/insights/secrets-successful-science-projects/) (search snippet)
- Steve Spangler Science advises framing results as supported or unsupported rather than right or wrong, and explaining why results differed instead of manipulating data. — [Steve Spangler Science, "Science Fair 911 – What If My Results Are Wrong?"](https://www.stevespanglerscience.com/?p=6614) (search snippet)
- A pre-college handbook states: "If your results do not support your hypothesis, that's ok and in some cases good!" and "never alter results to fit a theory." — [Richmond County (GA) Handbook for Science and Engineering Project](https://www.rcboe.org//cms/lib/GA01903614/Centricity/Domain/4248/Handbook%20for%20Science%20and%20Engineering%20Project.pdf) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- A "loophole-closing" null result (a pre-registered robustness check that also fails) is strongest when framed as a **positive methodological finding**, for example "Test X has a false-alarm rate of ~100% under conditions Y". It should not be framed as "my fix didn't work." Framed this way it scores on Research Question (a contribution to the field), Execution (reproducibility, appropriate statistics) and Creativity (a new perspective on an accepted method).
- Pre-registration, documented in the research plan addendum and dated before running, directly supports "systematic data collection and analysis" and protects against any appearance of p-hacking. Students should state this explicitly on the poster and in the interview.
- Interview preparation should cover: why the published test fails (mechanism), the scope and limits of the claim (which conditions or data-generating processes were tested), what was *not* shown, and concrete next steps. These map onto the "limitations" and "further research" interview bullets.
- These are reasoned inferences from the rubric text, not stated ISEF policy.

### Gaps
- I found no Society for Science or ISEF judge-authored source that specifically discusses null results, replication or failure-analysis projects, or critiques of published methods. I also found no data on how such projects place at ISEF.
- I found no ISEF guidance on presenting a "negative" mathematical result (a counterexample or a disproof). The math rubric question is unresolved (see Q1 gaps).
