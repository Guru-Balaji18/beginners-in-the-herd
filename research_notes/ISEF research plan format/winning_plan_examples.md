# Winning ISEF / STS research plans, project summaries and abstracts: structure and format (focus: MATH, BEHA, statistics, quantitative finance/economics)

Research method note: about 22 search/fetch calls (Firecrawl search + scrape). Pages actually opened and read are marked "(opened)". Pages known only from a search-result snippet are marked "(snippet only)". Nothing below is invented; where something could not be found it is listed under Gaps.

## Q1. Are actual research plans of ISEF winners publicly available? Where? Concrete examples

### Takeaway
Full Research Plans / Project Summaries (Form 1A attachment) of ISEF winners are almost never published. What is public is (a) the official 250-word abstract for every finalist in the Society for Science abstracts database (2014-2026), (b) poster boards on the ISEF virtual project-board site (isef.net), and (c) occasionally a student's GitHub README or notebook, which reads like a paper or project summary rather than the SRC-approved plan. I found no complete, verifiably ISEF-winning Research Plan document online in this search. Templates and blank instructions (and state-fair "sample" handouts) are plentiful.

### Cited Findings
- The official ISEF abstracts database (abstracts.societyforscience.org) is searchable by keyword, finalist last name, category (incl. "Mathematics", "Behavioral and Social Sciences"), country/state, and year (2014 through 2026), and has a filter for "Only Winning Abstracts". It exposes abstracts only, not research plans. (opened) — [ISEF Projects Database](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/)
- Each abstract page shows: title, Booth ID (e.g., MATH030), category, year, finalist name(s) and school, the abstract text, "Awards Won", and a "Print PDF" link (e.g., `/Home/PrintPdf?projectId=26951`). (opened) — [Example abstract page MATH030](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=26951)
- The ISEF virtual board site (isef.net, run on the projectboard.world platform per its favicon/asset host) lists "2026 Finalist Projects" with per-project pages (e.g., `isef.net/project/beha024---vr-meditation-on-stress-and-mindfulness`) and category filters such as `isef.net/viewAll?category=MATH-2024`. The scraped index text did not say what each entry contains (board, abstract, video); no research plans were visible. (opened) — [isef.net All Boards](https://isef.net/viewAll); [MATH category view](https://isef.net/viewAll?category=MATH-2024) (snippet only)
- ISEF rules say the Research Plan "should be completed before experimentation", must say which parts were done by student vs. mentor if a mentor was involved, and changes go in a "research plan addendum"; after an affiliated fair the project "may not be changed or amended". This is an SRC/compliance document, which explains why it is rarely published. (opened) — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- Concrete student-posted example (closest thing to a public plan): GitHub repo "Regeneron-ISEF-Finalist-Brain-Connectivity-in-Autism-with-ML" (bradyzhou1), claims 2026 Regeneron ISEF Finalist. Files include `2026 Cobb Paulding Regional Fair.ipynb`, `2026 GSEF Science Fair.ipynb`, `2026 Regeneron ISEF.ipynb`, data/sample CSV, `pipeline.png`. README headings: Project Overview, Dataset, Methodology, Machine Learning Models, Key Results (Leave-One-Site-Out Validation; Random Train/Test Validation), Major Findings, Repository Contents, Reproducibility, Awards, Motivation, Future Work, Citation, Disclaimer. The README has no research plan, abstract or hypothesis section. (opened) — [GitHub repo](https://github.com/bradyzhou1/Regeneron-ISEF-Finalist-Brain-Connectivity-in-Autism-with-ML)
- The regional-fair notebook in that repo contains a "Research Plan:" cell written as a short bulleted task list (e.g., build functional-connectivity features from `rois_cc200` .1D files; compute and plot group mean connectivity matrices; compare ASD vs Control; evaluate ...). (snippet only) — [2026 Cobb Paulding Regional Fair.ipynb](https://github.com/bradyzhou1/Regeneron-ISEF-Finalist-Brain-Connectivity-in-Autism-with-ML/blob/main/2026%20Cobb%20Paulding%20Regional%20Fair.ipynb)
- A Scribd document titled "ISEF Research Plan Sample" exists, described as guidance on what to include in an ISEF research plan; not opened (Scribd generally requires login), and its provenance/award status is unknown. (snippet only) — [Scribd: ISEF Research Plan Sample](https://www.scribd.com/document/428823835/ISEF-Research-Plan-Sample)
- Regional fairs publish blank sample forms incl. the research plan instructions (e.g., West Georgia RSEF sample forms PDF). (snippet only) — [WGRSEF sample forms](https://www.westga.edu/assets/wgrsef/docs/sample-forms.pdf)
- Reddit r/ISEFinalists notes that a sponsor teacher and the local fair SRC must sign off on the research plan (community source, not authoritative). (snippet only) — [r/ISEFinalists](https://www.reddit.com/r/ISEFinalists/)

### Inferences
- For modeling "what a winning plan looks like", the realistic public evidence is: official instructions + fair templates (structure) and winning abstracts (tone, density, quantitative reporting). Any claim of a "winning research plan" document online should be treated cautiously unless it links to the abstract database entry.
- Student GitHub repos are organized like short papers (Overview, Data, Methods, Results, Future Work, Reproducibility), not like the A-G research-plan template; they show how finalists present computational work after the fact.

### Gaps
- No complete, verified Research Plan from an ISEF Grand Award winner or STS finalist (2018-2026) was found. Searches for phrases from the template ("Rationale" "Expected Outcomes" "Data Analysis" "Bibliography") returned no student documents.
- Regeneron STS: I did not find public STS research reports or plans in this search (STS requires a research report, not the ISEF form; not verified here).
- Did not open isef.net individual board pages, so I cannot say whether boards include abstract text or downloadable files.
- Science News Explores / Student Science winner articles were not reached within the call budget.

## Q2. What do ISEF Grand Award abstracts in MATH and BEHA look like? Examples involving statistics, finance, economics, markets, data analysis

### Takeaway
Winning MATH/BEHA abstracts are a single block (sometimes 4-5 short paragraphs) of roughly 200-250 words (rule maximum 250), with no headings, no equations, no citations (apart from an occasional "et al." dataset credit), and no figures. They follow a fixed arc: problem and why it matters, gap in existing methods, purpose/hypothesis, data and method (named techniques, named datasets), quantitative result(s) (a statistic, test name, or percentage), and implications plus a limitation. First person ("I hypothesize", "we develop") is acceptable.

### Cited Findings
- Rule: "After experimentation, each student or team must submit a (maximum) 250-word, one-page abstract"; it must describe research done by the student(s), not adults. (opened) — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- ISEF 2025 Mathematics Grand Awards include: 1st, MATH028 "The Prisoner's Dilemma: Strategy Dynamics Related to Memory and Selfishness" (Cory Seelenfreund, New Rochelle HS, NY); 4th, MATH040T "Making Every Component Count: Using the Shapley Value To Improve Win Ratio Analysis" (Sophia and Valerie Fu, Carmel HS, IN); 4th, MATH034 "Modeling X-ray and Natural Images: Perceptually Relevant Image Statistics and Applications" (Vicky Cai, Stuyvesant). BEHA 4th includes BEHA065 "Modern Redlining: Quantifying the Causal Effects of American Zoning Law on Spatial and Multidimensional Urban Inequality" (Chisato Tarui, Iolani School, HI). (opened) — [Regeneron ISEF 2025 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)
- The same 2025 list shows BEHA Grand Awards are dominated by ML/neuro/mental-health/AI projects (e.g., BEHA035 CLIMB fMRI; BEHA041T deepfake detection; BEHA061 incel behavior on Reddit); a keyword scan of all 2025 Grand Award titles for finance/stock/market/economics/trading/portfolio found no finance-titled Grand Award winners; statistics/regression/forecasting appeared in other categories (BMED031, BCHM030, EAEV014T, EAEV048, PHYS045). (opened; scan done by the page-query tool) — [2025 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)

Example abstracts (all opened):

1. **MATH030 (2025), "Modeling Stock Price Dynamics With Student-t Random Walks: A Segment-Matching Approach for Improved Financial Analysis"**, Chiara Willimann, Kantonsschule Sursee (Switzerland). Finalist; "Awards Won" field was blank on the page. Five short paragraphs: (1) importance of stock-price models and the neglect of tail risk, project aim; (2) data (historical Swiss stock exchange data) and parameter fitting with reported statistics (kurtosis = 9.34; Student-t df = 4.642); (3) a novel segment-matching algorithm (trapezoidal area between paths over all windows); (4) evaluation by Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, headline result "improves the tail modeling by 70%"; (5) conclusion and contribution. — [MATH030 abstract](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=26951)
2. **MATH028 (2025, 1st Award $6,000), "The Prisoner's Dilemma: Strategy Dynamics Related to Memory and Selfishness"**. One paragraph. Motivation (selfish vs altruistic behavior is hard to study with human samples), gap ("debates remain"), method (Python packages), then results organized as "step 1 / step 2 / step 3" (reactive-1/2/3 memory strategies; complex interactions; blends of altruism), closing with real-world implication. Qualitative results; no numbers or test names. — [MATH028 abstract](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=27017); award from [2025 Full Awards](https://www.societyforscience.org/press-release/regeneron-isef-2025-full-awards/)
3. **MATH040T (2025, 4th Award), "Making Every Component Count: Using the Shapley Value To Improve Win Ratio Analysis"** (team). Biostatistics/game theory. One paragraph: context (composite endpoints in clinical trials), limitations of existing methods listed, gap ("provides limited insight into the contributions of individual components"), named contribution with acronym (SEWRA, Shapley-Enhanced Win Ratio Analysis), validation "through simulations and case studies", impact statement. Uses "we". — [MATH040T abstract](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=27423)
4. **BEHA065 (2025, 4th Award), "Modern Redlining: Quantifying the Causal Effects of American Zoning Law..."**. Economics/econometrics. One paragraph in first person: defines a construct ("spatial capital"), states two hypotheses explicitly ("I hypothesize that spatial capital adhere's [sic] to Piketty's r > g dynamic..."), names method (difference-in-differences causal inference), natural experiment (Minneapolis 2040 zoning reform), comparison design (five midwestern cities over four ACS time periods), results (reduced racial and income segregation, more housing affordability), and an explicit limitation ("short post-reform period"). — [BEHA065 abstract](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=27461)
5. Contrast example from a computational category, **CBIO047 (2024, special award "Third Award of $1,000")**, Shaurya Agrawal: one dense paragraph naming data source ("perturb-seq data from the genome-wide study done by Replogle et. al"), benchmark models, three named methods (greedy vector-addition, constrained OLS, non-linear SQP), two metrics (cosine similarity, custom projection score), and quantitative result ("almost double the benchmark, and 90%"). — [CBIO047 abstract](https://abstracts.societyforscience.org/Home/FullAbstract?ProjectId=25708)

### Inferences
- Strong quantitative abstracts name the test or estimator (Kolmogorov-Smirnov, difference-in-differences, OLS, Shapley value), the dataset, and at least one number; the 1st-place MATH028 abstract shows numbers are not strictly required when the design is staged clearly.
- Mentioning a limitation (BEHA065) and a "novel" named method or acronym (SEWRA, segment-matching) recurs.
- Pure finance/markets topics are uncommon among recent MATH/BEHA Grand Awards; quantitative-finance work tends to appear as finalists (MATH030), with economics showing up via policy/causal-inference framing (BEHA065) or game theory (MATH028).
- Word counts above are my visual estimates (roughly 200-250 words each), not computed.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether MATH030 won any award (the "Awards Won" field was empty on the scraped page).
- Did not retrieve 2018-2024 MATH/BEHA Grand Award lists or abstracts for finance/econ topics (call budget); the database's "Only Winning Abstracts" filter with keywords like "stock", "economic", "market" would find more but requires form submission.
- Regeneron STS finalist abstracts on economics/finance were not retrieved.

## Q3. Formatting conventions that recur in strong research plans

### Takeaway
The official structure is fixed: Rationale; Research Question(s)/Hypothesis(es)/Engineering Goal(s)/Expected Outcomes; then "in detail" List of Materials, Procedures, Risk and Safety, Data Analysis; then Bibliography; plus six extra lettered items (a-f) for human-participant studies. Fairs commonly ask for an outline with lettered headings (A-G), complete sentences, at least 5 reputable sources in APA. Extra sections such as timelines, variable tables, Gantt charts or H0/H1 notation are not required by ISEF and I found no primary evidence of how often winners add them.

### Cited Findings
- Official Form 1A instructions (2025): "The Research Plan is to be written prior to experimentation"; items: a. RATIONALE (background synopsis, importance, societal impact); b. RESEARCH QUESTION(S), HYPOTHESIS(ES), ENGINEERING GOAL(S), EXPECTED OUTCOMES ("How is this based on the rationale described above?"); c. Describe in detail: List of materials; Procedures ("Detail all procedures and experimental design including list of materials, methods for data collection, and when applicable, the source of data used. Describe only your project."); Risk and Safety; Data Analysis ("Describe the procedures you will use to analyze the data/results"); d. BIBLIOGRAPHY ("List major references ... from your literature review"). (opened) — [ISEF 1A Research Plan Instructions (2025)](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2025/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- Human participants add: a. Participants (age, gender, racial/ethnic composition; vulnerable populations); b. Recruitment; c. Methods (surveys/tests, permissions, frequency and length); d. Risk Assessment; e. Protection of Privacy (identifiable data, storage, access, disposal); f. Informed Consent Process. Relevant to BEHA survey/experiment projects. (opened) — [same PDF](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2025/Forms/1A-Student-Checklist-Research-Plan-Instructions.pdf)
- The current rules list "List of Materials" as its own required item and limit a project to 12 months of continuous research. (opened) — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- Southern Appalachian SEF (Univ. of Tennessee) instructs: type it, "Write your Research Plan in complete sentences and in an outline format", "use at least 5 reputable source, and follow APA formatting", team members each submit a copy, and gives a lettered template: A. Rationale; B. Research Question(s); C. Hypothesis(es) or Engineering Goal(s); D. Procedures including clean up and disposal if applicable; E. Risk & Safety; F. Data Analysis; G. Bibliography; header fields Name(s), Project Title, School, Date; same form reused as "Addendum". (opened) — [SASEF Research Plan](https://sasef.utk.edu/research-plan/)
- Society for Science's student guidance lists creating a timetable, making a research plan ("a roadmap of the questions you will have to answer"), constructing a hypothesis, testing one variable at a time and repeating tests; it says the final research paper should include title, table of contents, hypothesis, background research, materials, procedures, data analysis, conclusions, bibliography. (snippet of the page text) — [Society for Science: The Basics](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/the-basics/)
- Commercial guidance (sciencefaircenter.com) lists plan elements: question and hypothesis, purpose, "The variables you will change and measure". (snippet only) — [Science Fair Planning for Students](https://sciencefaircenter.com/science-fair-planning-for-students/)

### Inferences
- For a computational/statistics plan, "List of Materials" is normally a software/data table (language, libraries with versions, datasets with source and date range, hardware), and "Risk and Safety" is short (no physical hazards; data privacy/licensing). "Procedures" as numbered steps and "Data Analysis" naming specific tests, significance level and validation scheme fit the instruction to describe procedures "in detail" and how data "will" be analyzed, but this is my inference, not a quoted rule.
- Optional sections that align with the official text (and so are safe to add): Expected Outcomes (named in item b), source of data (named in Procedures), timeline (recommended by Society for Science's "Create a timetable"), and a student-vs-mentor delineation (required when a mentor was involved).
- H0/H1 notation, variables-and-controls tables and Gantt charts are coaching conventions; I found no source showing winning plans use them.

### Gaps
- No primary source on typical page length of winning plans; the official instructions give no page limit.
- Citation style: only fair-level guidance (SASEF: APA) found; ISEF itself does not mandate a style in the pages read.

## Q4. Expert or teacher guidance comparing weak vs strong research plans

### Takeaway
I found official checklists and fair instructions but no sourced side-by-side "weak vs strong research plan" comparison within the call budget. The best-supported quality signals come from the rules themselves (written before experimentation; plan must cover all phases; specify data source; mentor/student split) and from judging emphasis on creativity/originality and design/methodology.

### Cited Findings
- Society for Science lists judging criteria including "originality and creativity, design and methodology, knowledge achieved, and clarity of expression". (snippet of page text) — [The Basics](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/the-basics/)
- Science Buddies judging page: "One of the major criteria on a judges' list is creativity and originality." (snippet only) — [Science Buddies: Judging tips](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/science-fair/judging-tips-to-prepare-science-fair)
- The approved plan "must include any and all phases of the project to be conducted"; additional data may be collected only with "the same methodology that was previously approved". A vague plan therefore constrains later work. (opened) — [Rules for All Projects](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/)
- Society for Science publishes an SRC checklist that tells reviewers to "Look for answers that are consistent with the information on other forms". (snippet only) — [SRC Checklist](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/checklist-for-src-review/)
- Science Buddies' SRC guide frames approval as happening before research is conducted. (snippet only) — [Science Buddies: SRC How to Get Started](https://www.sciencebuddies.org/science-fair-projects/competitions/src-how-to-get-started)
- YRI Fellowship "How to Win ISEF in 2027" (commercial) was opened; the scraped content had no concrete research-plan or weak/strong advice, only that the official abstract is limited to 250 words. (opened) — [YRI ISEF guide](https://www.yriscience.com/blog/isef-guide)
- Attempted the official judging-criteria page at `/isef/judging-criteria/`: returned 404. (opened, failed) — [404 URL](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/judging-criteria/)

### Inferences
- A practical strong/weak rubric can be built from the official text: strong plans tie the question back to the rationale (item b asks this explicitly), name the data source and analysis procedure in advance, cover every phase that will be run, and are consistent with the other forms; weak plans are generic, omit the data source/analysis, or describe work done by mentors.
